# Campaign Update — August 18, 2026

## Our immediate priority is bringing the mortgage current

Receiving my son’s demand that we leave our home was shocking. This is the home Sascha and I chose, helped make possible, and have cared for since it was purchased as a new build in January 2023.

The first deadline in that demand has now passed. We were told that, by August 7, we had to produce a fully funded offer of approximately $350,000—backed by a financial institution—to purchase the home. Otherwise, according to the notice, Sascha, our cats, and I were expected to leave by August 31, 2026.

We could not produce that kind of institutional offer within ten days. We are still here, continuing to protect our family while we address the more immediate threat: the delinquent mortgage.

## How we reached this point

On March 7, 2026, my son left suddenly in the middle of the night, taking the household’s only car with him. In the letter he left, he said that he would continue supporting us while establishing a separate life. That promised support did not materialize, and we were left without the income structure on which our household had depended.

For ten years, Sascha and I had helped my son maintain the structure he needed to remain employed and participate in the IT business we operated together. We provided extensive unpaid labor, business support, practical help, and day-to-day accountability. Carrying that responsibility for so long exhausted our health, finances, and personal resources.

On September 13, 2023, the three of us signed a housing agreement. As we understand it, that agreement recognizes shared decision-making concerning the home and states that my son would not require us to leave before we could assume the title and mortgage in our own names.

Despite that agreement, we received a demand on July 28 giving us two choices: provide a fully funded purchase offer by August 7 or vacate by August 31.

## What happened with the mortgage

The central emergency is now the mortgage delinquency.

In mid-July, my son attempted to make three payments totaling approximately $8,000 to bring the loan current. Those payments, made on July 15 and 16, were later reversed. Based on the information available to us, he learned of the reversals around July 23.

Instead of asking us to work together on another solution, he decided that the property should be sold so that he could separate himself from what he called a “financial liability.”

We believe a sale would be difficult and financially damaging under the present circumstances. The amount owed on the mortgage is close to the home’s estimated market value. A sale could also involve preparation, moving, transaction, and closing costs. In addition, the solar system carries an approximately $44,500 obligation that we understand would need to be addressed as part of a sale or transfer. The unfinished backyard could create another substantial expense.

These factors do not determine what will happen, but they make a quick, uncomplicated sale far less realistic than the demand suggests.

## The step we took on August 11

On August 11, 2026, we recorded a notarized memorandum referring to our housing agreement and the equitable interest we assert in the property.

The recording creates a public record of our claimed interest and the underlying agreement. We understand that it may need to be addressed before a voluntary transfer can proceed. We are seeking qualified legal guidance concerning its precise effect, our occupancy rights, and the steps required to protect everyone’s lawful interests.

We do not want to overstate what a recorded document alone guarantees. What we can say is that we have formally documented our position and are no longer relying only on private promises.

## Two urgent mortgage targets before September 4

Whatever happens later with ownership or refinancing, the mortgage delinquency must be addressed if the home is to be protected from foreclosure.

We have now received a written mortgage notice stating that the company must receive **$8,028.12 by September 4, 2026** to bring the loan current. The notice warns that, if this amount is not received by that date, foreclosure and auction proceedings may begin at any time in September.

The mortgage company also told us that two full monthly payments are presently due and that May remains partially paid. According to the information provided to us, the minimum amount we need before September 4 is:

- **$2,471.86** toward May and June 2026; plus
- **$240.00** to complete the required amount;
- for a **minimum total of $2,711.86**.

We were told that receiving this minimum would reduce the delinquency from approximately 120 days to approximately 60 days. It would not bring the loan fully current, and it does not include the late fees that have accumulated since the default.

We have also been told that another forbearance may not be available until November 2027 because the prior forbearance began in November 2025 and must be followed by 24 completed monthly payments. Refinancing into our own names may ultimately be one path forward, but the options presented to us would require either a substantial down payment or a longer documented income history than we currently have.

That is why our fundraising priorities are now clear:

1. **Raise at least $2,711.86 in new, usable funds by September 4** to meet the minimum payment threshold described by the mortgage company and reduce the delinquency.
2. **Raise the full $8,028.12 by September 4** to satisfy the written amount required to bring the loan current and avoid the foreclosure-and-auction process described in the notice.

These urgent mortgage targets sit within our broader first milestone of $25,000, which also covers professional guidance, transportation, essential household costs, animal care, and the work required to stabilize our family. Our long-term campaign goal remains $500,000 because permanent safety may still require a fully financed transfer, a different permanent home, and enough stability to rebuild dependable income.

## What this is doing to Sascha—and why the cats must remain with us

The possibility of losing the home and being separated from our beloved cats is causing Sascha profound emotional distress. His cats are central to his daily stability and well-being. Their warmth, purring, touch, routines, and familiar presence help him endure relentless neurological pain.

The cats also need stability. A sudden relocation—or surrender to an overcrowded shelter—would expose them to fear, separation, and potentially grave outcomes. We adopted them as a lifetime commitment. We are their forever home, and we will continue doing everything lawfully possible to keep our family together.
