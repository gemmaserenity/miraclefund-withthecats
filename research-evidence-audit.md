## Evidence labels

- **Direct:** The cited source directly supports the wording.
- **Qualified:** The source supports the core point, but the report correctly limits certainty or generalizability.
- **Partial:** Only part of the sentence is supported by that source.
- **Inference:** A plausible application of adjacent evidence, clearly identified as inference rather than proven treatment.
- **Replace citation:** The claim is supportable, but the attached citation is mismatched, outdated, or weaker than a readily available source.

## Central conclusions that can remain

The following conclusions are supportable when expressed with the qualifications already present in the newer DOCX:

1. A bonded companion animal can be an important source of emotional support, routine, purpose, social connection, and pain-coping assistance.
2. Some human-animal-interaction research reports benefits, but findings are heterogeneous and are not universally positive.
3. Pleasant touch, distraction, perceived social support, and meaningful activity can influence pain experience or pain interference without curing the neurological source of pain.
4. Adults with spina bifida and myelomeningocele may face chronic pain and complex functional and psychosocial burdens.
5. A harmonious multi-cat household may offer relational diversity and greater continuity of companionship, but this is an inference rather than an established biological dose-response.
6. Cat purring is low-frequency and rhythmically salient, but controlled human trials have not established that ordinary cat purring heals bone, repairs tissue or nerves, treats neuropathic pain, or changes hydrocephalus or Chiari anatomy.
7. Cats do not reduce cerebrospinal-fluid accumulation, ventricular enlargement, intracranial pressure, or cerebellar/brainstem displacement.
8. Risks involving falls, scratches, bites, litter hygiene, zoonoses, sleep disruption, cost, care burden, and multi-cat stress should remain in the report.

## Authoritative sources added during gap searching

The following sources were located after auditing the active links and should be incorporated into the final reference set:

- Stensland ML, McGeary DD. *Use of animal-assisted interventions in relieving pain in healthcare settings: A systematic review.* Complementary Therapies in Clinical Practice. 2022;46:101519. PMID: 34894530. This supports describing animal-assisted intervention as promising for pain relief while emphasizing that the available evidence is weak and methodologically heterogeneous.
- Wagner C, Grob C, Hediger K. *Specific and Non-specific Factors of Animal-Assisted Interventions Considered in Research: A Systematic Review.* Frontiers in Psychology. 2022;13:931347. PMID: 35837630; PMCID: PMC9274084. This supports caution about attributing observed benefits specifically to an animal rather than social interaction, novelty, therapeutic attention, or context.
- Bert F, et al. *Animal assisted intervention: A systematic review of benefits and risks.* European Journal of Integrative Medicine. 2016;8(5):695–706. PMID: 32362955; PMCID: PMC7185850. This provides broad evidence concerning potential pain, stress, and anxiety benefits alongside infection, allergy, and injury risks; most included interventions were not cat-specific.
- Spina Bifida Association. *Guidelines for the Care of People with Spina Bifida.* This is the preferred source family for adult care burdens involving mobility, pain, skin integrity, bladder and bowel care, mental health, self-management, participation, care coordination, and quality of life.
- Stevens JA, Teh SL, Haileyesus T. *Dogs and cats as environmental fall hazards.* Journal of Safety Research. 2010;41(1):69–73. PMID: 20226954. This should replace the mismatched PMID currently attached to the fall-risk sentence.
- Ellis SLH, et al. *AAFP and ISFM feline environmental needs guidelines.* Journal of Feline Medicine and Surgery. 2013;15(3):219–230. PMID: 23422366; PMCID: PMC11383066. This is the preferred veterinary source for separated resources, safe places, play, predictable interaction, and stress reduction in multi-cat environments.
- *Co-sleeping with pets, stress, and sleep in a nationally representative sample of United States adults.* PMCID: PMC10918166. This supports a nuanced account: co-sleeping effects vary by animal and outcome, and a greater number of pets was associated with greater insomnia severity in that study.
- Yeh TL, et al. *A modest protective association between pet ownership and cardiovascular diseases: A systematic review and meta-analysis.* PLOS ONE. 2019;14(5):e0216231. PMCID: PMC6499429. This should accompany or replace wording that currently describes a single observational cat-ownership study as a meta-analysis; the pooled conclusions were cautious and did not establish causality.
- NINDS. *Hydrocephalus* and *Chiari Malformation.* These are the preferred sources for symptom descriptions and the need for medical evaluation. They do not support claims that cats or purring alter intracranial pressure, CSF production, ventricular size, or hindbrain anatomy.
- CDC. *Cats: Healthy Pets, Healthy People*, *About Giardia and Pets*, and current Bartonella guidance. These are the preferred sources for zoonotic-risk and hygiene language. The Giardia section should explicitly state CDC's conclusion that people are unlikely to acquire Giardia from dogs or cats.
