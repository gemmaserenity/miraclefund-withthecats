Our story

Our home is at risk. So is the family that makes it home.

We are Gemma and Sascha, and we are asking for help to keep our family of cats safe, stay together, and reach a stable home in Arizona.

The cats have been at the center of our lives for as long as either of us can remember. We grew up with them, found comfort in them, cared for them, and mourned them. To us, they have never been possessions. They are our children: distinct little lives with whom we share years of love, trust, routine, and belonging.

For Sascha, that bond carries another dimension. He lives with myelomeningocele, unshunted hydrocephalus, Arnold–Chiari II malformation, and severe neurogenic pain that never switches off. The cats' warmth, purring, gentle pressure, familiar presence, and daily routines help him regulate stress, redirect attention from pain, sleep, and remain engaged when a flare makes ordinary activity difficult. Each cat knows his body, his boundaries, and his quiet signs of distress.

♥

This is what we are fighting for: a safe home where Sascha can manage his disability and every cat can remain with the family they know—not a forced separation when they are needed most.

How we arrived here

We came to the United States to rebuild a life after years marked by domestic violence and upheaval. Sascha—an American citizen who knew he would never have biological children because of his disability—made that move for Gemma and her children. When an adult family member later needed a safe place and intensive support, we opened our home and structured our lives around helping him build one.

For nearly ten years, that care shaped every part of our household. It also made consistent income and the growth of our own business much harder. When we bought our home in Maricopa in 2023, the financing relied on the family member whose conventional employment qualified for the mortgage, while a cohabitation agreement documented our shared stake in the home.

Then, at 2:34 a.m. on March 7, 2026, our family structure changed overnight. That family member left without warning. The household and shared-business income we had relied upon stopped, and the financial support promised in the letter left behind did not materialize. We suddenly had to sustain ourselves, the mortgage, utilities, and every cat without predictable revenue.

We did not stop. We rebuilt our work to operate virtually, learned new technologies, and created the foundations of new digital businesses. We are doing everything we can to become self-sustaining. But building those systems takes time, and the legal and financial control of our current house does not rest with us alone. We now face losing it before our new work has had time to carry us.

Why an ordinary rental is not a real solution

Most rentals restrict the number of animals allowed. Splitting up the cats would fracture a bonded family and remove the continuous circle of companionship that helps Sascha through unpredictable pain and bedbound periods. For us, securing a home we can control is not a luxury—it is the only durable way to protect the whole family and prevent this crisis from repeating.

Your support will buy time, cover immediate living and cat-care costs, fund a safe move if one becomes necessary, and help us secure permanent housing where no one can force us to choose which members of our family get to stay.

Any amount moves us closer to safety. If you cannot give, sharing this page with someone who understands that animals can be family—and vital disability support—is an equally meaningful act.

With gratitude,
Gemma, Sascha, and the cats

More than companionship

Why the cats are essential to Sascha’s daily life

Sascha’s neurological conditions can bring relentless burning, electric, and pressure-related pain, disrupted sleep, limited mobility, and deep fatigue. The cats cannot cure those conditions. What they provide is continuous, familiar support that makes the symptoms more bearable.

  • Calming sensory input: purring, warmth, touch, and familiar weight can help redirect attention from pain and ease stress.
  • Support through flare-ups: different cats stay near him at different times, providing companionship without exhausting a single animal.
  • Routine and purpose: feeding, care, and interaction create structure even on days dominated by pain.
  • A bond that cannot be replaced: these cats have learned Sascha’s movements, sensitive areas, and subtle signs that he is struggling.

These are Sascha’s lived experiences and supportive benefits; they are not presented as a cure or a substitute for professional medical care.
