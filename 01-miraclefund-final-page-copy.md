# He Promised We Would Be Safe—Now My Disabled Husband’s Life Is at Risk

I am Gemma Serenity Gorokhoff.

For years, my adult son repeatedly promised that he would take care of us and that Sascha and I had nothing to worry about. He told us that we were family and that the home was our shared security.

We trusted those promises.

For ten years, my husband, Sascha, and I organized our work, finances, transportation, sacrifices, and future around the security he assured us we would have. We helped him remain stable enough to work, preserve his employment, serve clients, and build the IT business whose income supported our household.

We gave him our time, unpaid labor, practical support, emotional protection, security, and faith because he was family.

Then, at 2:34 a.m. on March 7, he left with the household’s only car. The employment and client income upon which our family had depended was no longer available to us. Financial assistance described in the letter he left behind did not materialize.

Now, after everything we gave and everything he promised, he is acting for his own financial interests and demanding that Sascha, our beloved cats, and I leave the home we fought to secure.

He is doing this with full knowledge that Sascha is profoundly disabled, lives with severe and relentless neurological pain, and depends upon stability to protect his already fragile health.

He also knows that our beloved cats are Sascha’s entire emotional world. Their warmth, purring, touch, familiarity, and constant presence are the only things that consistently help ease the distress caused by his disability.

Removing Sascha from his home and separating him from the animals he loves places his physical and psychological health in grave danger. For Sascha, this creates a genuine risk of catastrophic deterioration and death.

We are not asking for luxury.

We are asking for safety, permanence, and the chance to keep every member of our family—human and feline—together.

# WHY THIS IS A MEDICAL AND FAMILY EMERGENCY

## The danger is not theoretical—and the cats are not possessions.

Sascha was born with myelomeningocele, unshunted hydrocephalus, Arnold–Chiari II malformation, and severe neuropathic pain.

His body already works every day under an extraordinary neurological burden. His pain may feel burning, electrical, crushing, or pressure-related. It disrupts sleep, limits movement, drains concentration, and can make ordinary activity almost impossible.

Extreme stress does not remain confined to his emotions. It affects his sleep, pain levels, concentration, muscle tension, blood pressure, and ability to function. Housing instability and the terror of losing his beloved animals are placing additional strain on a system that already has very little reserve.

The medical background linked on this page explains how severe stress, sleep disruption, displacement, and the sudden loss of an established support system can seriously worsen Sascha’s neurological condition. For him, this creates a genuine risk of catastrophic deterioration and death.

I know my husband. I see what this fear is doing to him.

I see his pain intensify.

I see him lose sleep.

I see the exhaustion, panic, and despair that come from imagining himself homeless and separated from the cats who are his entire emotional world.

The person demanding that we leave knows Sascha is disabled. He knows how severe the pain is. He knows what these cats mean to him. And he is proceeding anyway.

## The cats are Sascha’s everything.

Our beloved cats are not decorations around Sascha’s life. They are woven into nearly every hour of it.

When pain confines him to bed, they remain beside him.

When he cannot sleep, their familiar warmth and purring help calm him.

When his world becomes reduced to pain, they give him something loving outside his own body to focus upon.

They provide routine when pain destroys the shape of the day. They give him purpose when disability makes him feel powerless. They approach him without judgment, impatience, or seeing him as a medical problem.

They simply love him.

Different cats have formed different bonds with him. They recognize his movements, sensitive areas, breathing, pain behaviors, boundaries, and the quiet moments when he needs companionship.

The cats cannot cure Sascha’s disabilities, and they do not replace medical care. But in Sascha’s lived experience, their collective presence is the only nonmedical form of comfort that has consistently helped ease the distress surrounding his pain.

They are his children.

They are his companions.

They are the beings who remain beside him when his body becomes unbearable.

To force him from his home is already cruel. To force him to choose which members of his family he must lose would be devastating beyond words and could cause profound psychological and physical harm to an already medically vulnerable man.

They know us as home. We will not willingly betray their trust.

# HOW OUR FAMILY ARRIVED HERE

## Ten years of sacrifice—and the damage left behind

I came to the United States hoping to rebuild my life after years marked by domestic violence and upheaval.

Sascha—an American citizen who knew that his disability meant he would never have biological children—welcomed my children as his own. He reorganized his life around protecting our family and helping us build a future.

From the time my son was 18 until he was 28, Sascha and I repeatedly placed his stability ahead of our own.

We helped him through recurring periods of crisis and destabilization.

We helped him preserve employment, serve IT clients, organize his work, rebuild routines, and regain his footing whenever his life became unmanageable.

We also helped scaffold and operate the IT business behind the scenes.

Much of the invisible structure that enabled my son to work and earn came from our unpaid labor inside the home and behind the business.

We did it because he was my son.

We believed family meant helping someone stand until he could stand independently.

Throughout those years, my son continued to assure us that he would take care of us and that we had nothing to worry about. Those assurances reinforced our decision to keep placing his stability ahead of our own and to keep investing our labor and future in the household.

But the emotional cost was profound.

Over time, our household became shaped by conflict, volatility, intimidation, and uncertainty.

We learned to measure our words.

We learned to anticipate reactions.

We learned to absorb one crisis after another because we feared what might happen if everything escalated.

We experienced this pattern as emotionally abusive.

Abuse is not limited to a single dramatic event. It can also be the slow destruction of safety—the feeling that your home is no longer peaceful, that another person’s reaction controls the atmosphere, and that your own needs must continually disappear to prevent the next crisis.

The damage accumulated over years.

For Sascha, who already lives with severe congenital neurological conditions and relentless pain, prolonged fear and instability placed an additional burden on his body.

Stress intensified his suffering.

Sleep became more fragile.

Rest became harder.

The home that should have protected him increasingly became a place where he could not fully relax.

For me, it meant years of remaining vigilant—trying to protect my husband, support my son, preserve the household, keep the business operating, and stop everything from collapsing.

Our own businesses, income, savings, health, emotional well-being, and long-term security repeatedly had to come second.

When we purchased our home in Maricopa in 2023, my son’s employment qualified the household for the mortgage.

Our unpaid labor at home and inside the business helped make that income possible.

We believed we were building a shared foundation where the people who had worked, sacrificed, and supported one another would remain safe.

Then, at 2:34 a.m. on March 7, 2026, my son quietly left with only essential belongings—but also with the household’s car and the active sources of revenue: his employment income and the IT clients whose work we had helped support.

The financial assistance described in the letter he left behind did not materialize.

Sascha, our cats, and I were suddenly left without transportation and without the income structure around which our household had been built.

The departure did not merely create a financial shortfall.

It destabilized nearly every part of our lives at once.

It left a disabled man facing extreme uncertainty.

It left me trying to rebuild income while protecting him and caring for our entire feline family.

It left animals who depend completely upon us vulnerable to decisions they cannot possibly understand.

For five months, we survived because compassionate people brought money, groceries, transportation, household supplies, urgent bill payments, cat food, and litter.

Food banks, churches, pet-support organizations, utility-assistance programs, friends, and acquaintances helped keep our family alive while we rebuilt our work virtually, learned new technologies, and laid the foundations for new digital businesses.

Their compassion kept us going.

Then the crisis became even more severe.

On July 28, 2026, we received a written notice requiring us either to vacate by August 31, 2026, or to produce by August 7, 2026, a fully funded purchase offer covering the entire mortgage and all closing costs.

Separately, the mortgage was scheduled to enter the pre-foreclosure period on August 1, 2026, because my son had not made the mortgage payment he had promised to make to secure our house. After leaving, he had referred to the property in writing as “Gemma and Sascha’s home.”

We immediately activated our network to prevent the mortgage situation from progressing further.

Sascha and I secured access to approximately **$9,000**, enough to address the stated mortgage arrears and bring the loan current.

We asked my son to accept those funds, bring the loan current, and give us time to complete a properly financed transfer into our own names.

On August 3, 2026, he declined.

He stated that the only outcome he expected was to sell the house. He also stated that no mediation or other recourse would be considered. The sole exception he offered was a fully funded purchase offer backed by a financial institution by August 7, 2026.

These were his own written words to the parents who had sacrificed ten years of their lives to preserve his interests in every possible way.

This is the injustice at the center of our campaign:

My son promised that he would take care of us.

He assured us that we had nothing to fear.

For ten years, we helped him remain stable, employed, housed, supported, and able to earn. We gave him unpaid labor, practical support, emotional protection, business assistance, and sacrifices that came directly from our own future security.

Now that protecting us conflicts with his own financial interests, those promises appear to mean nothing.

He is demanding that we leave despite knowing that Sascha is profoundly disabled, that extreme stress worsens his suffering, and that losing his home and bonded feline family places his health and life in grave danger.

He is not merely asking us to change addresses.

He is threatening the only environment in which Sascha feels physically and emotionally safe.

He is threatening to separate a profoundly disabled man from the beloved animals who are his comfort, purpose, family, and—in the darkest hours of pain—his reason to keep going.

The consequences are already visible. Sascha’s pain, sleep, concentration, and ability to function have suffered under prolonged fear. The energy he needs to rebuild our livelihood is being consumed by the terror of losing his home and the animals he loves as children.

I am trying to hold together a family whose world has been destabilized by someone we repeatedly protected.

Our beloved cats cannot understand a mortgage, a family rupture, or why their home might suddenly disappear. They only know that we are their people. They expect us to protect them.

We cannot look into their faces and decide that some are worth saving while others must be abandoned.

We will not willingly betray their trust.

We need a permanent home that no one can suddenly take from beneath us again.

# WHY AN ORDINARY RENTAL IS NOT A REAL SOLUTION

Most conventional rentals impose strict limits on animals.

A large, closely bonded indoor feline family cannot simply be placed in an ordinary apartment.

Separating the cats would fracture relationships built over years. It would remove the continuous circle of companionship that supports Sascha through unpredictable pain, sleepless nights, and periods when he is confined to bed.

Temporary accommodation could force us to surrender some of the animals, divide them among unfamiliar homes, or move repeatedly from one precarious arrangement to another.

That would be devastating for Sascha.

It would also be deeply traumatic for animals who know one another, know our voices, recognize our routines, and trust that we will remain together.

Securing a home we can control is not a luxury.

It is the only durable way to protect the whole family and prevent this crisis from repeating.

A cash purchase offers what another fragile arrangement cannot:

- Speed and certainty before the deadline.
- No mortgage-qualification delay while our businesses are still growing.
- A stable and accessible home for Sascha.
- A lawful and permanent home for our entire bonded feline family.
